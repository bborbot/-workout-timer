Power

Short, fast efforts: 20–30 seconds, up to about 20 reps, with a light-to-moderate load of roughly 20–40% of your maximum. The goal is speed and explosiveness, not grinding.

Author's preference

Multi-joint, full-body movements through a large range of motion — not isolated exercises for a single muscle group. Special emphasis on the legs: jumps of all kinds. Kettlebell, bodyweight, plates, a barbell or a resistance band all work. Don't start the next set until your breathing has fully recovered.

Examples
  • Legs: band-resisted jumps, jumps from a full squat — lower slowly with a weight, then drive up explosively; squat jumps, broad jumps, box jumps, skater jumps
  • Kettlebell: swing, snatch, clean and press, goblet squat to press
  • Bodyweight: burpees, plyometric push-ups
  • Plate: plate thrusters, plate push press, plate swings
  • Barbell: push press, thrusters, hang power clean, landmine press (one end of the bar on the floor)
  • Medicine ball: slams, wall balls, rotational throws
Tips

Every rep as fast as you can with good form. When the speed drops, the set is over — even before 30 seconds. Set the rest long enough for your breathing to recover; if it doesn't, make the rest longer. With age, rest usually needs to be longer.

This is the author’s personal view, not sports or medical advice. If you have health problems or injuries, or the workouts are for a child, ask a doctor or a coach first.
