Stretching

Slow, calm stretches held in place. You gradually get back range of motion in the joints, release tension after hard work and help recovery. If you feel stiff as a board, this is exactly for you — progress comes slowly, over weeks, but it comes.

Author's preference — when

After Strength and Power, at the end of the session. It can also go before swimming — in that case the swim is for full relaxation: an easy pace, no sprints.

How

Move into the stretch slowly until you feel mild tension — never pain. Hold for 20–40 seconds and breathe calmly; with each exhale, let the muscle relax a little more. 2–3 times per position. Don't bounce.

Examples
  • Back of the legs: seated forward fold, standing hamstring stretch (heel on a low step)
  • Front of the legs and hips: standing quad stretch, kneeling hip flexor stretch
  • Hips and glutes: pigeon pose, figure-four stretch lying on your back, butterfly
  • Calves: wall calf stretch
  • Back: child's pose, cat–cow, lying spinal twist
  • Chest and shoulders: doorway chest stretch, cross-body shoulder stretch, overhead triceps stretch
Tips

The muscles stretch best when they are warm — after a workout or a warm-up, not cold. If you are very stiff, start with 5–6 positions and 20 seconds, and add a little each week. Consistency beats intensity: 10 minutes 3 times a week works better than an hour once a month. The timer default (45 seconds of stretch, 15 seconds to change position) fits this well.

This is the author’s personal view, not sports or medical advice. If you have health problems or injuries, or the workouts are for a child, ask a doctor or a coach first.
