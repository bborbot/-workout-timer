Speed & Reaction

Very short, very fast efforts of 5–15 seconds with long rest. You train quick feet, quick hands and a fast response to a signal — not strength and not endurance.

Author's preference

Light, fast and sharp: reaction drills with a tennis ball, ball dribbling, fast footwork on a ladder or rings, fast light punches on a heavy bag. Don't start the next set until your breathing has fully recovered.

Examples
  • Tennis ball: wall rebound catch, drop-and-catch (a partner drops the ball, you catch it after one bounce), reaction ball (an uneven ball that bounces unpredictably)
  • Basketball: fast dribbling — in place, around the legs, with two balls
  • Agility ladder (marked on the floor): quick feet, in-and-out, lateral shuffle, Ickey shuffle
  • Agility rings: hopscotch-style hops on one and two feet
  • Heavy bag: speed punches — light, as fast as possible, not hard
  • Short sprints of 5–10 metres from different starting positions
Tips

Maximum speed only while you are fresh: when you get slower, stop and rest. Work 5–15 seconds, rest much longer — about 5 times the work, and in any case until your breathing has recovered. Where possible, react to a signal (a partner, a bounce, a clap) rather than a pattern you know by heart.

This is the author’s personal view, not sports or medical advice. If you have health problems or injuries, or the workouts are for a child, ask a doctor or a coach first.
