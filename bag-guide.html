Endurance

There are two kinds of endurance, and both matter: muscular endurance — the intervals in this app, and endurance in minutes — long steady activity for the heart and breathing.

Muscular endurance — in this app

The same movements as in Power, but lighter and longer: the load is 10–20% lower than in Power, and each set lasts about 30–40 seconds. You train the muscles to keep working when they are tired.

Author's preference. Multi-joint, full-body movements, as in Power. Work until you feel the burn in the muscles, then keep going for about 5 more seconds — 2–5 more reps — and stop. Rest is incomplete: start the next set before you have fully recovered. With age, be careful with this — watch your breathing and your heart rate, and lengthen the rest if needed.

Examples

The same as in Power:

  • kettlebell swings, thrusters, squat jumps, burpees
  • push press, plate swings, wall balls, heavy bag rounds
Endurance in minutes — heart and breathing

Long, steady activity at a comfortable pace, counted in hours per week. WHO recommends at least 150–300 minutes a week of moderate activity.

Author's preference. Walking — 5–10 hours a week, or swimming — about 4–5 hours a week.

This is well above the WHO minimum — build up to it gradually over weeks. Log walks and swims on the main page with + and the number of minutes.

Tips

Keep a steady pace and good form; when the form breaks, the set is over. Breathe rhythmically, don't hold your breath.

This is the author’s personal view, not sports or medical advice. If you have health problems or injuries, or the workouts are for a child, ask a doctor or a coach first.
