Strength

Lifting heavy loads with good technique. Each session follows the same pattern, and the load changes from week to week: heavy weeks for maximal strength, medium weeks for strength and muscle endurance, and once a month a light “marathon” session.

Author's preference — the week

About 2 strength sessions a week, each for different muscle groups — not the whole body every time. Multi-joint exercises, different ones in each session.

Author's preference — the session: back-off sets
  1. Warm-up sets — 2–3 sets of the same exercise with a light weight.
  2. One top set — the heavy weight of the week, as many good reps as you can.
  3. 1–2 back-off sets — the weight 20–30% lower. That's it.

Don't start the next set until you feel you have recovered.

Author's preference — the month

The weight of the top set changes over the month:

  1. Maximal strength — about 90% of your one-rep maximum, 1–3 reps.
  2. Strength and endurance — about 70% of your maximum, 8–10 reps.
  3. “Marathon” — once a month — about 50% of your maximum, 12–15 reps.

A typical month: 1–2 weeks of type 1, 1–2 weeks of type 2, one session of type 3.

Author's preference — legs and deadlift

Squat in a hack squat machine rather than with a free barbell: with age, free squats with a heavy weight put the back at risk. A hack squat and other leg machines (leg press and the like) are quite enough for serious weights.

Combine the deadlift with the Romanian deadlift. The Romanian deadlift takes the front of the legs out of the work and loads the back of the legs; the deadlift works everything together — that matters. Increase the deadlift weight very carefully: start at about 40% of your maximum and don't add weight until your technique is solid.

Examples
  • Legs and back: hack squat, leg press, deadlift, Romanian deadlift, lunges
  • Push: bench press, overhead press, dips
  • Pull: pull-ups, rows, lat pulldown
  • Kettlebells: goblet squat, kettlebell press, kettlebell row
Tips

The timer default is 40 seconds of work and 2 minutes of rest — but rest as long as you need: don't start the next set until you have recovered. At 90%, technique comes first: if a rep is slow and ugly, stop. With age, or without a coach, start heavy weeks at 80–85% rather than 90%, and use a spotter or safety bars for the bench press. Don't train strength on consecutive days.

This is the author’s personal view, not sports or medical advice. If you have health problems or injuries, or the workouts are for a child, ask a doctor or a coach first.
